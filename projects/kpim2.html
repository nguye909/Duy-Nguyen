The Problem & Context

What business questions needed to be answered?

The capstone focused on understanding Christmas-season retail performance from transaction-level data. The analysis needed to show how revenue, quantity, cost, profit, and margin changed by season and month, then explain performance through customer, product, location, purchase, payment, budget, weekday, and hour-of-day patterns.

My Role

What I owned

I developed the SQL workflow from source inspection through final analysis. I standardized the raw schema, corrected the customer-age field, converted time values safely, created Christmas-season logic, excluded an incomplete season, calculated reusable KPIs and growth measures, and built business queries across the major analytical dimensions.

Methodology

How I approached the analysis

STEP 01

Review source tables and fields, correct naming issues, safely convert time data, define seasonal date logic, and create an analysis-ready SQL layer.

STEP 02

SQL Evidence

How the raw data became analysis-ready

;WITH xmas_sales AS
(
    SELECT
        CASE
            WHEN MONTH([date]) = 1
                THEN YEAR([date]) - 1
            ELSE YEAR([date])
        END AS xmas_year,

        CASE
            WHEN MONTH([date]) = 1
                THEN CONCAT(YEAR([date]) - 1, '-', YEAR([date]))
            ELSE CONCAT(YEAR([date]), '-', YEAR([date]) + 1)
        END AS xmas_season,

        YEAR([date]) AS [year],
        EOMONTH([date]) AS end_date_of_month,
        DATEPART(WEEKDAY, [date]) AS [weekday],

        CASE DATEPART(WEEKDAY, [date])
            WHEN 1 THEN 'Sunday'
            WHEN 2 THEN 'Monday'
            WHEN 3 THEN 'Tuesday'
            WHEN 4 THEN 'Wednesday'
            WHEN 5 THEN 'Thursday'
            WHEN 6 THEN 'Friday'
            WHEN 7 THEN 'Saturday'
        END AS weekday_name,

        DATEPART(HOUR, [time]) AS [hour],
        *

    FROM dbo.xmas_sales
    WHERE EOMONTH([date]) <> '2018-01-31'
)
Analysis preparation · Created Christmas-season and time dimensions while excluding the incomplete 2017–18 season to support consistent sales analysis.

Example Business Queries

From raw transactions to decision-ready outputs

Analysis 01

Compare Christmas seasons, not just calendar years

The project assigns January transactions to the preceding Christmas season, allowing November, December, and January activity to be evaluated as one retail season instead of splitting the business event across calendar years.

Analysis 02

Separate revenue growth from profitability

The SQL tracks sales, quantity, cost, profit, and profit margin so higher revenue can be evaluated alongside whether the business actually generated better economic performance.

Analysis 03

Identify where and when performance is generated

Location, customer, product, purchase, payment, weekday, and hourly queries make it possible to identify which segments and shopping periods drive the strongest results.

Business Value

How the analysis supports decisions

The SQL workflow creates a consistent analytical foundation for answering seasonal retail questions: which products and markets contribute the most, whether growth is profitable, which customer groups matter, and when customers are most active. The reusable clean view also reduces the need to repeat transformation logic across every query.
